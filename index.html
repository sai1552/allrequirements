  • Proof that the company accepted your resignation and agreed your last working day. Use it if the relieving letter is delayed.

  • Official proof that you’ve been relieved from your duties and your resignation is accepted.

  • Shows your job title, duration of employment, and sometimes a brief on your role.

  • Issued by the employer for tax filing. It shows the total salary and tax deductions. Make sure you get both parts.

  • Details the full and final payment calculation including salary, bonus, leave encashment, etc.

  • Official proof of your salary payments for recent months.

  • Useful for applying for loans or future employment. Includes your PF account number, UAN and last contribution details. Confirm your PF is transferred to your new employer (Form 13) rather than withdrawn.

  • Confirms that you’ve returned company assets and cleared all dues. Required for background checks.

  • Legal document related to confidentiality or any post-exit obligations. Keep it for your records.

  • If you had corporate health insurance, keep documents related to policy closure or transfer.

  • New employers often ask for these during background verification. Collect copies of all of them in one go.

  • If you’re leaving before your full notice period ends, get the waiver or buyout terms in writing.

  • If you signed a training or service bond, get written confirmation that it’s closed.

  • If you received a joining bonus, get written confirmation that no clawback is due.

  • If the company gave you any equity or NPS contributions, collect the latest statements.

  • Applies only if you’ve completed 5 years with the company.

  • Copies of any certificates issued by the company.

  • The HR email or phone number future employers should use to verify your employment.

  • Gather references and contact information for future verification or re-employment.
